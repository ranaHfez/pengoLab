🧮 Add Tens and Ones on an Open Number Line

enVision Math · Grade 2 · Volume 1 · Topic 3 · Lesson 3-2

🎯 Objectives & Vocabulary

I can add a two-digit number and a two-digit number by drawing jumps of tens and then jumps of ones on an open number line.

Standards: 2.NBT.B.5, 2.NBT.B.6, 2.NBT.B.9 · Time: 45 minutes

open number linetensonesjumpsumaddstrategy

Materials: whiteboards and markers, base-ten blocks, sticky notes, chalk/tape number line, tablet or screen for the games below.

⏱️ Lesson Flow (45 min)
MinStageWhat to do
5Warm-UpPlay Bunny Hop as a class. Students show answers on whiteboards, then jump +10 and +1 on a floor number line.
10Explore (Solve & Share)Problem: “Mia has 34 stickers. Her friend gives her 25 more. How many now?” Pairs solve with blocks or a drawing, then share.
10Model itDraw an open number line (no fixed ticks). Start at 34. Break 25 into 20 + 5. Jump +20 to 54, then +5 to 59. Say: “Tens first, then ones.”
5Guided practiceDo 3 more together: 27 + 31, 45 + 28 (ones cross a ten), 52 + 36. Students draw jumps on whiteboards.
12Game stationsRotate teams through Frog Pond, Rocket Jump, Monster Bakery (about 4 min each).
3Exit ticketSolve 43 + 26 on an open number line and write one sentence explaining the jumps.
📏 Model: 34 + 25

Key idea: the line is “open” because we only mark the numbers we need.

🧠 Teacher Tips, Misconceptions & Differentiation
  • Misconception: jumping +2 instead of +20. Ask “How many tens?”
  • Misconception: jumping the tens digit only (34 + 25 → 54 + 2). Break the whole addend: 25 = 20 + 5.
  • Support: use base-ten rods beside the line; start with sums that do not cross a ten (34 + 25) before ones that do (38 + 25).
  • Challenge: break the ones too: +2 to reach the next ten, then +3. Or ask “Can you solve it in a different order?”
  • Check for understanding: “Why did we jump 20 and not 2?” “Where did the +5 start?”
