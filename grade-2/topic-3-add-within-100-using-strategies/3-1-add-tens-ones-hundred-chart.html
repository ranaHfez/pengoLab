🦄 Add Tens and Ones on a Hundred Chart 🌈

enVision Math · Grade 2 · Volume 1 · Topic 3: Add Within 100 Using Strategies · Lesson 3-1

🎯 Lesson Overview

Time about 45 minutes   Standard 2.NBT.B.5, 2.NBT.B.9

Learning goal: Students add two-digit numbers by moving on a hundred chart: down for tens, right for ones.

Essential question: How can a hundred chart help me add tens and ones?

Vocabulary: tens, ones, hundred chart, add, sum, row, column.

Materials: large hundred chart, counters, markers, devices for the games, mini whiteboards.

📋 Step by Step

1. Warm-up (7 min) → Play Tens and Ones first so students remember to count the tens, then the ones. Then play Ten More, Ten Less. Ask: "What happens to the tens digit? The ones digit?"
2. Explore (8 min) → Show 24 + 13 on the Whiteboard (hundred chart background). Start at 24. Move down 1 row (+10) to 34, then right 3 (+3) to 37. Students predict first, then check.
3. Teach (10 min) → Model 36 + 25: down 2 rows = 56, right 5 = 61 (the chart wraps to the next row). Write 36 + 20 = 56, 56 + 5 = 61. Students copy on mini whiteboards.
4. Guided practice (5 min) → Give 3 problems: 18 + 30, 42 + 16, 57 + 24. Partners explain "down for tens, right for ones."
5. Games (15 min) → Groups rotate through Games 1 to 4.
6. Exit ticket (2 min) → "Show 45 + 32 on a hundred chart. Where do you land?" (Answer: 77)

💬 Questions to Ask

  • Which way do you move to add tens? Why?
  • What happens when the ones go past the end of the row?
  • Could you add the ones first? Do you get the same answer?
  • How can you check your answer another way?

Differentiation: Support: use only tens first (23 + 30). Challenge: add three numbers (14 + 20 + 25).

✏️ Whiteboard

Tip: with 👆 Move, tap any number to place the frog, then tap another number to hop, or use ⬇️ +10 for tens and ➡️ +1 for ones. Switch to 🖊️ Pen to draw arrows on top.
