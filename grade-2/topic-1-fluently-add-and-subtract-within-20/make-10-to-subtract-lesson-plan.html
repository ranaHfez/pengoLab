Make 10 to Subtract

enVision Math · Grade 2 · Topic 1 · Lesson 7 · about 50 minutes

⭐ 0

Learning goal

Students subtract from a teen number by first taking away to reach 10, then taking away the rest. Example: 14 − 8 → 14 − 4 = 10, then 10 − 4 = 6.

Vocabulary

make 10, ten frame, subtract, difference, break apart

Materials

ten frames, two-color counters, number line 0–20, whiteboards, this page on a screen

Prior skills

Partners of 10, subtracting within 10

Lesson flow

5 minWarm-up: Ten Partners
10 minExplore: pairs solve 15 − 9 with counters and share how they did it
10 minTeach: model the strategy (demo below)
20 minGames 1–4 in stations or as a whole class
5 minExit ticket

Teach: try 14 − 8

Ask: Why is 10 a helpful stopping point? Have students say each step aloud: “14 minus 4 is 10. 10 minus 4 is 6.”

Common mistakes

Taking away the full number from the ones only (14 − 8 → 4 − 8). Forgetting to subtract the “rest.” Counting the ten-frame dot that was already crossed out.

Support and stretch

Support: keep ten frames and counters, use problems where the ones digit is 1 less than the number subtracted. Stretch: ask students to write their own make-10 subtraction story.

Exit ticket

13 − 9 · 16 − 8 · 12 − 7

Warm-up: Ten Partners

Tap the number that makes 10. Get 8 right to finish. (Teacher: say the partner facts out loud together.)

Game 1: Ten-Frame Takeaway

Cross out the extra ones first to make 10. Then keep crossing out until you have taken away the whole amount.

Game 2: Hop to 10

Hop back to 10, then hop the rest of the way. Answer each step.

Game 3: Balloon Pop

Solve the problem, then pop the balloon with the answer. Pop 6 to win. Tip: make 10 first!

Game 4: Fix the Steps

Fill in the missing numbers. Solve 5 to win.
