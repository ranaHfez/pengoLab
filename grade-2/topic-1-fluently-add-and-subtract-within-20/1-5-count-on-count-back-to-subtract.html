Lesson 1-5: Count On and Count Back to Subtract

Standard: CCSS 2.OA.B.2 Duration: ~45 minutes Grouping: Whole group → partners → independent
Lesson at a glance

1 Objective & focus

Students will subtract fluently within 20 by choosing an efficient counting strategy — counting back when the number being subtracted is small, or counting on when the two numbers are close together — and will justify which strategy they picked.

Essential question: How can counting on or counting back help you subtract?

Step 2 · Teach (8–10 min)

3 Mini-lesson: Pick your path

Model on the board with a large number line or the hop tool above. Introduce the idea that both directions solve subtraction — the trick is picking whichever one takes fewer hops.

Count back ⬅

Best when the number you're subtracting is small.
Example: 12 − 3 → start at 12, hop back 3 times: 11, 10, 9. Answer: 9.

Count on ➡

Best when the two numbers are close together.
Example: 14 − 12 → start at 12, hop on to 14: 13, 14 — that's 2 hops. Answer: 2.

Think-aloud prompt for the class: "Are these numbers close together, or is the number I'm subtracting small? Which path has fewer hops?"

  1. Write a problem (e.g., 15 − 2). Ask: close together, or small subtrahend?
  2. Have students show thumbs up (count back) or thumbs sideways (count on).
  3. Solve together on the number line, counting hops out loud.
  4. Repeat with 2–3 more problems, gradually releasing to student volunteers.
Step 3 · Guided Practice Game (10 min)

4 Pick the Path Interactive

Students choose the smarter strategy first, then hop to solve. Play whole-class on the board, then let pairs take turns at a shared device. Correct strategy + correct answer both earn points.

Step 4 · Independent Practice (8–10 min)

5 Workbook & partner check

Students complete enVision Student Edition p. 1-5, items 1–12 independently. Fast finishers move to the Facts Blitz game below. Circulate and pull a small group for reteach using ten-frame counters for anyone still counting all instead of counting on/back.

  • Support: provide a printed 0–20 number line and counters for physically marking hops.
  • On level: complete workbook page, checking answers with a partner using the "close together or small number?" question.
  • Extension: write one count-on and one count-back problem of their own for a partner to solve, using numbers up to 20.

Step 6 · Wrap-Up (3–5 min)

7 Closure & exit ticket

Ask 2–3 students to share which strategy they used most today and why. Then hand out (or display) this quick exit ticket:

  1. Solve: 16 − 2 = ___. Which strategy did you use?
  2. Solve: 13 − 11 = ___. Which strategy did you use?
  3. In your own words: when is counting back easier than counting on?

Use responses to sort tomorrow's small groups: reteach (still counting all), practice (inconsistent strategy choice), extend (ready for Lesson 1-6).
