🔟 Make 10 to Add

Grade 2 · enVision Math · Addition Strategy Lesson

⏱ 45–50 min 📐 CCSS 2.OA.B.2 👥 Whole Group → Partners → Independent
Objectives Materials Warm-Up I Do We Do Interactive Practice Ten Partners Game You Do Exit Ticket Differentiation

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Students will use the make-10 strategy to add two numbers when one addend is 8 or 9 (e.g., 8 + 5, 9 + 6).
  • Students will explain, using a ten-frame, how "breaking apart" the smaller number helps make a ten.
  • Students will fluently write the equation that shows the strategy (e.g., 8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13).

Success Criteria

"I can fill a ten-frame to make 10, then add what's left over to solve an addition problem within 20."

🧰 Materials

Teacher

  • Large magnetic ten-frame + counters
  • enVision Math Topic (Make 10 to Add) student edition
  • Anchor chart paper & markers
  • This interactive lesson (projected)

Students

  • Individual ten-frame mats (1 per student/pair)
  • 20 two-color counters per student
  • Whiteboard + marker
  • enVision workbook page for the lesson

🔥 Warm-Up 5 min

Quick Ten-Frame Flash: Show a ten-frame filled with 8 dots for 3 seconds, hide it, and ask: "How many? How do you know it's 8 and not 10?" Repeat with 7 and 9.

"Turn and tell your partner: how many more dots would we need to make a full ten-frame of 10?"

Chart 2–3 responses. This previews the "how many more to make 10" thinking students need for today's strategy.

👩‍🏫 I Do — Direct Instruction 10 min

  1. Write 8 + 5 = ? on the board. Ask: "Is there a friendly number nearby that would make this easier?" Guide students to notice 10 is a friendly number.
  2. Model on the large ten-frame: place 8 counters in the frame (2 empty spots left).
  3. Say: "I have 8 in my frame. I need 2 more to make 10. My other number is 5 — I can break 5 into 2 and 3."
  4. Move 2 counters from the "5" group into the frame to make 10. 3 counters remain outside.
  5. Write the equation chain step-by-step: 8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13
  6. Underline: "10 + 3 is easy — it's just 13!"

Anchor Chart Steps (post for the lesson)

  1. Find the number close to 10 (usually 8 or 9).
  2. Ask: "How many more to make 10?"
  3. Break apart the other number to give that many.
  4. Add 10 + the leftover.

✏️ You Do — Independent Practice 10–12 min

Students complete the enVision workbook practice page independently, using their own ten-frame mat and counters to show work for at least 3 problems before writing equations.

Practice Set

8 + 4    9 + 3    8 + 9    9 + 8    8 + 6    9 + 5

Early finishers: Challenge card — solve 9 + 9 and 8 + 8 using make-10, then write a real-world story problem for one equation.

📝 Exit Ticket / Formative Check 5 min

On an index card, students solve 9 + 6 by drawing a ten-frame and writing the equation chain (9 + 6 = 9 + 1 + 5 = 10 + 5 = 15).

Quick Check-in Signals

  • 👍 Got it: Correctly fills the frame and chains the equation.
  • 👌 Getting there: Fills the frame correctly but struggles to write the equation chain.
  • 👎 Reteach: Needs small group to revisit filling the ten-frame concretely.

🌈 Differentiation

Support

  • Use pre-filled ten-frames (dots already drawn) so students only add the leftover counters.
  • Keep numbers within 8/9 + small numbers (2–5) before moving to larger leftovers.
  • Pair with a peer helper; use physical counters over pictures.

Extension

  • Introduce making 10 with two-digit addends (e.g., 18 + 5).
  • Have students explain make-10 for subtraction (think-addition).
  • Challenge: find all addition facts equal to 13 using the make-10 strategy.
