Even and Odd

Make pairs. If nothing is left over, the number is even. If one is left over, it is odd.

enVision Math · Grade 2 Topic 2: Work With Equal Groups Lesson 2-1

6 is even

3 pairs, none left over. 3 + 3 = 6

7 is odd

3 pairs and 1 left over.

Lesson plan

Follow the student book’s Solve & Share, Visual Learning, Guided Practice, Independent Practice and Problem Solving pages alongside this plan. Page numbers and exact problems can differ by edition.

Learning goal

Students decide whether a group of up to 20 objects is even or odd by making pairs. They write an even number as the sum of two equal addends.

Big question

How can I tell if a number is even or odd?

Vocabulary

even, odd, pair, equal groups, addend, ones digit

Materials

  • Two-color counters or linking cubes
  • Ten-frames
  • Number cards 1 to 20
  • Whiteboards and markers
  • Student book, tablets or a projector

Standards and practices

CCSS 2.OA.C.3 (odd and even numbers, equal addends). Math practices: MP3 explain your thinking, MP7 look for structure.

Time

About 50 minutes. Games can move to math centers or a second session.

  1. Warm-up game 5 min

    Play Pencil Match on the board. Students pair up pencils, then say if the number is even or odd.

    Teacher script

    “Pencils come in pairs. Tap two pencils to make a pair. Keep going. Is any pencil left over?”

    After each round ask: “How many pairs? How many left over? Is the number even or odd?”

  2. Solve & Share 7 min

    Use the book’s opening problem. If you want a backup story:

    “Sam has 9 crayons. He wants to put them in pairs. Can he make pairs with no crayon left over?”

    • Students use counters or drawings to try it.
    • Pairs share what happened and how they know.
    • Repeat with 8 crayons and compare the two results.

    📝 Notes board

  3. Visual Learning: even and odd 10 min

    • An even number makes pairs with none left over. It can be shown as two equal groups.
    • An odd number makes pairs with one left over.
    • An even number is a sum of two equal addends, like 5 + 5 = 10. An odd number cannot be.
    • Look at the ones digit. Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8. Odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9.
    • Counting by 2s from 2 lands on even numbers.

    Try the Even and Odd Explorer. Tap any number from 1 to 20.

    Even ones digits: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8Odd ones digits: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9

  4. Guided Practice and Convince Me 8 min

    • Students do the Guided Practice items with a partner. They use counters to make pairs and check each answer.
    • Convince Me prompts you can use: “Is 13 even or odd? Draw pairs to show how you know.” and “Write 14 as two equal addends.”
    What to listen for

    Strong answers mention pairs and a leftover, or give an equation like 7 + 7 = 14. Some students may also point to the ones digit.

  5. Game centers 12 min

    Rotate small groups through the four games, about 3 minutes each. Game 4 also works well as a whole-class mini-lesson on the board.

    • Rocket Sort: decide if a number of objects is even or odd.
    • Equal Groups Builder: write even numbers as two equal addends and spot numbers that cannot.
    • Number Detective: use the ones digit, find odd and even numbers, and skip count by 2s.
    • Last Digit Trick: see that only the ones digit decides if a number is even or odd, even for big two-digit numbers (31 to 99).

  6. Independent Practice and Problem Solving 5 min

    Students complete the book’s Independent Practice and Problem Solving items. Circulate and ask: “How do you know?”

    Higher-order prompt: “Maya says 15 is even because 15 = 7 + 8. Is she right?” (No. 7 and 8 are not equal addends, and 15 leaves one left over.)

  7. Exit ticket 3 min

    • Is 11 even or odd? Draw pairs to show.
    • Write 12 as the sum of two equal addends.
    • Circle the even numbers: 5, 8, 14, 17.
    Answer key

    1) Odd: 5 pairs and 1 left over. 2) 6 + 6 = 12. 3) 8 and 14.

Support every learner

Needs support

  • Use real counters and numbers up to 10.
  • Draw a circle around each pair.
  • Say “pairs, then leftover” every time.

On level

  • Work with numbers up to 20.
  • Write an equation for every even number.

Ready for more

  • Use the ones digit for numbers up to 100.
  • Explore: add two even numbers. Is the sum even or odd? What about two odd numbers?

I can statements

  • I can make pairs to tell if a number is even or odd.
  • I can write an even number as two equal addends.
  • I can use the ones digit to check.

Quick check

Watch for students who think any addition sentence makes a number even. Remind them the two addends must be equal.

Home connection

Ask your child to count the pencils or toy cars at home and say if the total is even or odd.

Warm-up game: Pencil Match ✏️

Skills: making pairs, finding a leftover, connecting pairs to even and odd.

How to use: whole class on the board, or pairs on tablets. 5 rounds.

Game 1: Rocket Sort 🚀

Skills: deciding if a group of up to 20 objects is even or odd.

How to use: 10 questions. After each answer the objects are shown in pairs so students can check.

Game 2: Equal Groups Builder 🧱

Skills: writing an even number as the sum of two equal addends; knowing an odd number cannot be split into two equal groups.

How to use: 8 questions. Students can try again after a wrong answer, but only first tries earn stars.

Game 3: Number Detective 🔎

Skills: using the ones digit, finding odd and even numbers, skip counting by 2s, adding 1 to change odd and even.

How to use: 8 mixed questions. Students can try again after a wrong answer, but only first tries earn stars.

Game 4: Last Digit Trick 🔦

Skills: an even or odd number is decided by its ones digit only. The tens and hundreds digits do not matter. This game uses two-digit numbers from 31 to 99.

How to use: first explore the Digit Table together, then play 10 questions. Students can try again after a wrong answer, but only first tries earn stars.

Digit Table: look at the last digit

Tap any number. Look at its ones digit, the last digit. Each column has the same ones digit and the same color.
