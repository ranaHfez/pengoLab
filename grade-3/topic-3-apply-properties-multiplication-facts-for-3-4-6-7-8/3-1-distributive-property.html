Break it apart, then multiply

The Distributive Property lets you split a big array into two small arrays you already know.

enVision Math · Grade 3 Topic 3 Lesson 3-1
7 × 4 = (5 × 4) + (2 × 4)
= 20 + 8 = 28

Lesson plan

Follow the student book’s Solve & Share, Visual Learning, Guided Practice, Independent Practice and Problem Solving pages alongside this plan. Page numbers and exact problems can differ by edition.

Learning goal

Students use the Distributive Property to break an array into two smaller arrays, find each part with facts they know, and add the two products to find the whole product.

Big question

How can I break apart an array to help me multiply?

Vocabulary

Distributive Property, array, factor, product, break apart, parentheses

Materials

  • Grid paper and two colored pencils
  • Square tiles or counters
  • Scissors (to cut paper arrays)
  • Whiteboards and markers
  • Student book, tablets or a projector

Standards and practices

CCSS 3.OA.B.5 (apply properties of operations) and 3.OA.C.7 (fluently multiply within 100). Math practices: MP3 explain and compare ideas, MP7 look for structure.

Time

About 50 minutes. Games can move to math centers or a second session.

  1. Warm-up game 5 min

    Play Array Splitter on the board. Students tap a line to break an array into two arrays, then add the two parts.

    Teacher script

    “Here is a big array. Where could we cut it so each piece is easier to count? Tap a line and see.”

    After each round ask: “What are the two small arrays? How did you find the whole?” Let students notice that different cuts give the same total.

  2. Solve & Share 7 min

    Use the book’s opening problem. If you want a backup story:

    “A bakery tray has 6 rows of 4 muffins. Break the tray into two smaller trays. How many muffins in all?”

    • Students draw the array on grid paper and color two parts.
    • Pairs share where they broke it apart.
    • Compare splits like 5 + 1 and 3 + 3. Ask: “Why do we get the same total?”

  3. Visual Learning: the Distributive Property 10 min

    • Break apart one factor into two smaller numbers, like 7 = 5 + 2.
    • Multiply each part by the other factor: 5 × 4 and 2 × 4.
    • Add the two products: 20 + 8 = 28.
    • Parentheses show which part to do first: 7 × 4 = (5 × 4) + (2 × 4).
    • Choose parts that are facts you know, like 2s, 5s and 10s. Any split works.
    • You can break apart either factor.

    Try the Array Splitter Explorer. Pick the rows and columns, then slide the cut.

  4. Guided Practice and Convince Me 8 min

    • Students do the Guided Practice items with a partner. They draw and color the array, then write the equation.
    • Convince Me prompts you can use: “Break 7 × 6 into 5 × 6 and 2 × 6. Why does that give the same answer as 7 × 6?” and “Show a second way to break apart 8 × 4.”
    What to listen for

    Strong answers say the two small arrays together make the big array, so the total stays the same. Listen for “the parts add up to the whole.”

  5. Game centers 12 min

    Rotate small groups through the three games, about 4 minutes each.

    • Break Apart: match arrays to equations and fill in missing numbers.
    • Product Race: use two small arrays to find the whole product.
    • Distributive Detective: word problems, true or false, and “who is right?”

  6. Independent Practice and Problem Solving 5 min

    Students complete the book’s Independent Practice and Problem Solving items. Circulate and ask: “Which facts did you use?”

    Higher-order prompt: “Marco says 6 × 7 = (3 × 7) + (3 × 7). Ana says 6 × 7 = (5 × 7) + (1 × 7). Who is right?” (Both. 21 + 21 = 42 and 35 + 7 = 42.)

  7. Exit ticket 3 min

    • Fill in: 8 × 3 = (5 × 3) + (3 × 3) = __ + __ = __
    • Draw a line to break apart a 6 × 4 array. Write an equation.
    • True or false: 7 × 4 = (5 × 4) + (2 × 4).
    Answer key

    1) 15 + 9 = 24. 2) Answers vary, such as 6 × 4 = (5 × 4) + (1 × 4) = 24. 3) True: 20 + 8 = 28.

Support every learner

Needs support

  • Use grid paper and two colors.
  • Start with arrays up to 6 × 4 and break apart at 5.
  • Say “break, multiply, add” each time.

On level

  • Work with arrays up to 9 × 6.
  • Write the full equation with parentheses.

Ready for more

  • Break apart 8 × 7 in two different ways.
  • Challenge: try 9 × 6 as (10 × 6) − (1 × 6).

I can statements

  • I can break an array into two smaller arrays.
  • I can use facts I know to find each part.
  • I can add the parts to find the product.

Quick check

Watch for students who add the factors instead of the products, such as (5 + 4) + (2 + 4). Return to the colored arrays and count each part.

Home connection

Use an egg carton or muffin tin as an array. Cover some rows with paper and say the equation for the two parts.

Warm-up game: Array Splitter ✂️

Skills: breaking an array into two arrays, writing the broken-apart equation, adding the two products.

How to use: whole class on the board, or pairs on tablets. 5 rounds. Any cut is allowed.

Game 1: Break Apart 🧩

Skills: matching a broken-apart array to its equation, finding a missing number, finding a missing product.

How to use: 10 questions. Students can try again after a wrong answer, but only first tries earn stars.

Game 2: Product Race 🏁

Skills: using two small arrays and known facts to find a product within 81.

How to use: 8 problems. Students find each small array first, then add. Splits include 5 + something and equal halves.

Game 3: Distributive Detective 🕵️

Skills: word problems, choosing a helpful expression, true or false splits, breaking apart either factor, knowing that different splits give the same product.

How to use: 8 mixed questions. Students can try again after a wrong answer, but only first tries earn stars.
