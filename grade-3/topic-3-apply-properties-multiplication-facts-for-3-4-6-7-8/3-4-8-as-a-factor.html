Apply Properties: 8 as a Factor

🎯 Mission Objective

Students use properties of operations (commutative, distributive, associative) and the double-the-4s strategy to find 8 × n and solve problems with 8 as a factor.

8 × nDoublesBreak apartArrays

📖 Hero Vocabulary

FactorA number multiplied by another (8 and 6 in 8 × 6).
ProductThe answer to a multiplication problem.
Distributive PropertyBreak a fact into two easier facts: 8 × 6 = (4 × 6) + (4 × 6).
Commutative PropertyOrder doesn’t change the product: 8 × 3 = 3 × 8.
ArrayObjects in equal rows and columns.

🧩 Explore: The Gadget Array

Pick the number of columns. The red rows are 4 × n, the blue rows are another 4 × n.

💡 Strategies to Teach

1. Double the 4s

8 × 6 → 4 × 6 = 24 → double 24 = 48.

2. Break apart with 5 and 3

8 × 7 = (5 × 7) + (3 × 7) = 35 + 21 = 56.

3. Commutative swap

8 × 3 is the same as 3 × 8 = 24, which is easy to skip count.

4. Double, double, double

8 × 5: 2 × 5 = 10, double = 20, double = 40.

Common mistake: forgetting to double at the end. Ask: “How many fours are in 8?”
