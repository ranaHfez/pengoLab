Add in Any Order

Turn the train around and the sum stays the same. Tap the button and watch 3 + 4 become 4 + 3.

3 + 4 = 7

Same sum: 7

Lesson plan

I can add numbers in any order and get the same sum.
Standards
CCSS 1.OA.B.3 (order property of addition), 1.OA.C.6 (add within 20, fluently within 10)
Time
About 50 minutes. Phases are flexible, so split across two days if needed.
Key words
add, sum, turn-around fact, order, count on, same
Materials
Two-color counters or linking cubes (two colors), mini whiteboards and markers, number line 0 to 10, student book Lesson 2-5, a screen for this page

Lesson flow

Tap a phase to open the steps and teacher talk.

1Warm-up: Flip Flop Fact5 min

Do

  1. Open the warm-up game on the screen.
  2. Read the fact, let children say the sum with a thumbs-up when ready.
  3. Tap the answer. The cube train flips and a second sentence appears.
  4. Play 4 to 5 rounds as a whole class, then stop.

Say

"Look at the cubes. How many in all? Now the train flipped. What do you notice?"
"Still the same! Today we learn why."

Look for

  • Children who recount every cube instead of noticing the same sum.
2Explore: Solve and Share8 min
Story: "Mia built a train with 3 red cubes and 4 blue cubes. Ben turned the train around so the blue cubes come first. Does Ben have more, fewer, or the same number of cubes?"

Do

  1. Partners build Mia's train with two colors of cubes.
  2. They turn the train around and count again.
  3. Each child writes both number sentences on a mini whiteboard.
  4. Two or three pairs share at the board.

Sentence stem

"3 + 4 is the same as 4 + 3 because ___."

Ask

  • Did any cubes disappear or get added?
  • What changed? What stayed the same?
3Teach: Swap Lab10 min

Do

  1. Open the Swap Lab. Pick 2 and 6.
  2. Tap "Swap the groups". Read both sentences together.
  3. Tap "Count on". Start at the bigger number, count on the smaller group.
  4. Try 5 and 3, then 1 and 8. Let children choose the next pair.
  5. Build the anchor chart: Add in any order. The sum stays the same.

Say

"2 + 6 and 6 + 2 are turn-around facts. They have the same sum."
"Adding in any order helps us count on. Start with the bigger number, 6, then count on 7, 8."

Chant

"Turn it around, turn it around, the sum stays the same, turn it around!"
4Guided practice: Whiteboard8 min

Do (class board and mini boards)

  1. Use the Train maker under the whiteboard to add 5 + 2 and the swapped 2 + 5.
  2. Children draw 4 red and 3 blue circles, then write 4 + 3 and 3 + 4.
  3. Switch to the number line mat. Jump 2 then 5, then 5 then 2. Where do you land?
  4. Switch to the swap mat. Put the two groups in, then swap them.

Convince me

"Is 7 + 2 the same as 2 + 7? Show me with a drawing."

Look for

  • Children who think the first number must be bigger.
  • Children who count all from one instead of counting on.
5Practice: 4 games10 to 12 min

Do

  • Whole class: play one game on the screen, children answer on mini whiteboards.
  • Stations: groups of 3 to 4 rotate through the games on tablets or computers (about 3 minutes each).
  • Choose by readiness. See the table below.

The games

  • Turn-Around Builder: build the swapped sentence.
  • Partner Match: memory cards with turn-around facts.
  • Number Detective: find the missing number.
  • Balloon Pop: pop the turn-around partner.
6Quick check and close7 min

Do

  1. Give the Quick check (4 items) on paper or mini whiteboards.
  2. Exit ticket: write one pair of turn-around facts and draw cubes to show it.
  3. Chant the anchor line together.

Say

"What did we learn today? Tell your partner one turn-around fact."

Home connection

Ask your child to show you 2 groups of small toys and turn them around. "Is the total the same?"

Watch for

  • "3 + 4 and 4 + 3 are different." Use cubes and flip the train.
  • Doubles such as 4 + 4 have no different partner. Celebrate that as a fun exception.
  • Order does not work for subtraction. Keep this lesson on adding only.

Support and challenge

  • Support: sums within 5 first, real cubes, color-coded groups.
  • On level: all games, sums within 10.
  • Challenge: find every pair that makes 8 and write both orders.

Check your book

Teacher talk

"Two groups can go in any order. The total stays the same."
"Turn-around facts: 2 + 6 and 6 + 2."

Count on the easy way

Start with the bigger number, then count on the smaller group. For 2 + 6, say 6, then 7, 8. Swapping the order makes counting on shorter.

Ask the class

  • What stayed the same when we swapped?
  • Which order is quicker for counting on? Why?
  • When are the two sentences exactly alike?

Quick check and exit ticket

Use on paper or mini whiteboards. Teachers can show the answers.

  1. 2 + 6 = 8. So 6 + 2 = ___
    Answer: 8
  2. Write the turn-around fact for 4 + 1 = 5.
    Answer: 1 + 4 = 5
  3. Circle the sentence that matches 5 + 3 = 8:   3 + 5 = 8   5 + 4 = 9   3 + 3 = 6
    Answer: 3 + 5 = 8
  4. Convince me: Is 7 + 2 the same as 2 + 7? Draw cubes to show it.
    Answer: Yes. Both make 9. Drawings show the same cubes in a different order.

Exit ticket: Write one pair of turn-around facts. Draw cubes to show your pair.
