Doubles

A double adds the same number two times. Today we find doubles, build them, and add them.

enVision Math · Grade 1 Topic 2: Fluently add and subtract within 10 Lesson 2-2
top rowbottom row
3 + 3 = 6

Both rows match. That is a double!

Lesson plan

Follow the student book’s Solve & Share, Visual Learning, Guided Practice, Independent Practice and Problem Solving pages alongside this plan. Page numbers and exact problems can differ by edition.

Learning goal

Students add doubles from 1 + 1 to 5 + 5 using objects, drawings, ten-frames and equations. They explain that a double has two equal addends.

Big question

How can doubles help me add?

Vocabulary

double, add, equal, same, sum, equation

Materials

  • Two-color counters, ten-frames
  • Linking cubes, dominoes or dice
  • Whiteboards and markers
  • Student book, tablets or a projector

Standards and practices

CCSS 1.OA.C.6 (add within 10 fluently). Math practices: MP4 model with math, MP7 look for structure.

Time

About 50 minutes. Games can move to math centers or a second session.

  1. Warm-up game 5 min

    Play Ladybug Mirror on the board. Students tap spots on the right wing to match the left wing.

    Teacher script

    “This ladybug is a mirror. Both wings must match. How many spots on the left? Make the right wing the same.”

    After each round ask: “How many on the left? How many on the right? How many in all?” Listen for the words the same.

  2. Solve & Share 7 min

    Use the book’s opening problem. If you want a backup story:

    “Mia has 3 apples in each hand. How many apples does she have in all?”

    • Students solve with counters, cubes or a drawing.
    • Pairs share how they solved it.
    • Ask: “How are the two groups alike?” Guide them to say both groups are the same size.

  3. Visual Learning: what is a double? 10 min

    • A double adds two groups that are the same size.
    • On a ten-frame, a double has the same number in the top row and the bottom row.
    • Say each fact aloud: “Double 3 is 6.”
    • Find doubles in real life: two eyes, two hands, two shoes, two bike wheels.

    Try the Doubles Explorer. Tap a number and watch the ten-frame mirror it.

    1 + 1 = 22 + 2 = 43 + 3 = 64 + 4 = 85 + 5 = 10

  4. Guided Practice and Convince Me 8 min

    • Students do the Guided Practice items with a partner, using counters to check each answer.
    • Convince Me prompts you can use: “Is 4 + 4 a double? How do you know?” and “Is 3 + 4 a double? Draw to show why or why not.”
    What to listen for

    Strong answers say the two numbers are the same, or point to matching rows on a ten-frame. For 3 + 4, students may notice there is one extra counter.

  5. Game centers 12 min

    Rotate small groups through the three games, about 4 minutes each.

    • Balloon Pop: add doubles from 1 + 1 to 5 + 5 with pictures.
    • Double or Not?: tell doubles from other facts using ten-frames.
    • Double Stories: solve word problems, match pictures to equations, and find the missing double.

  6. Independent Practice and Problem Solving 5 min

    Students complete the book’s Independent Practice and Problem Solving items. Circulate and ask students to read each fact aloud: “Double __ is __.”

    Extension prompt: “Draw a picture for 5 + 5. How many in all?”

  7. Exit ticket 3 min

    • What is 4 + 4?
    • Draw two equal groups of 3. Write the equation.
    • Is 2 + 3 a double? Tell why.
    Answer key

    1) 8. 2) Two groups of 3 dots, 3 + 3 = 6. 3) No, because 2 and 3 are different numbers.

Support every learner

Needs support

  • Use real counters and one ten-frame.
  • Start with doubles 1 to 3.
  • Say “Double 2 is 4” together before writing.

On level

  • Work all doubles from 1 + 1 to 5 + 5.
  • Explain each answer with a picture or ten-frame.

Ready for more

  • Write a double story for a partner.
  • Preview: 3 + 4 is one more than 3 + 3. What is 3 + 4?

I can statements

  • I can say what a double is.
  • I can show a double with counters or a ten-frame.
  • I can add doubles to 10.

Quick check

Watch for students who add the numbers correctly but cannot say why a fact is a double. Revisit the ten-frame rows with them.

Home connection

Ask your child to find things that come in doubles at home, like two eyes or two socks, and say “Double 2 is 4.”

Warm-up game: Ladybug Mirror 🐞

Skills: the two addends in a double are the same; building a double with objects; reading the equation.

How to use: whole class on the board, or pairs on tablets. 5 rounds.

Game 1: Balloon Pop 🎈

Skills: adding doubles from 1 + 1 to 5 + 5 using pictures.

How to use: 10 questions. Students pop the balloon with the right sum and earn a star for each first-try answer.

Game 2: Double or Not? 🔍

Skills: recognizing doubles, matching ten-frame rows, explaining why 3 + 4 is not a double.

How to use: 10 cards, five doubles and five other facts, in a new order every time.

Game 3: Double Stories 📖

Skills: double word problems, matching a picture to its equation, finding the missing double from the total.

How to use: 8 mixed questions. Students can try again after a wrong answer, but only first tries earn stars.
