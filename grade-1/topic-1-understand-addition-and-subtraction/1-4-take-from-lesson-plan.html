Topic 1, Lesson 4 — Solve Problems: Take From

A 30-minute lesson plan built around interactive warm-up and practice games for subtraction "take from" stories within 10.

⏱ 30 minutes 🎯 Standard 1.OA.A.1 🧮 Subtraction to 10 👥 Whole group + partners

📌 Lesson Snapshot Big Idea

Students act out and draw "take from" situations (a starting amount, some taken away, an amount left) and represent them with subtraction equations, building on Lesson 3's "put together/take apart" work.

Learning Objective Students will solve "take from" word problems within 10 using objects, pictures, and a subtraction equation, and will explain what each number in the equation represents.
Success Criteria "I can act out or draw a take-from story, write a subtraction equation for it, and tell what the difference means."
Vocabulary take from · minus (–) · left · difference · equation · subtract
Materials Two-color counters or small objects (10 per student), mini whiteboards/markers, number cards 0–10, one die per pair, printable ten-frame mats

🗓️ 30-Minute Flow

0–5 min
Interactive Warm-Up

Game: "Disappearing Bugs"

Whole class, no materials needed — gets bodies and brains moving into subtraction thinking.

  • Everyone stands. Teacher calls out a start number (e.g., "10 bugs on the wall!"). Class echoes and holds up 10 fingers.
  • Teacher says "3 bugs flew away — squish them down!" Students lower 3 fingers while counting down out loud.
  • Class shouts the number left together: "7 bugs are left!"
  • Repeat 3–4 times with new start/take-away numbers (keep within 10).
Say: "We started with 10. 3 flew away. How many are left? Let's check by counting our fingers."
5–12 min
Mini-Lesson / Model

Visual Learning Bridge: Modeling a "Take From" Story

Use the enVision Visual Learning Bridge page (or recreate with counters + ten-frame on the board).

  • Tell a story: "8 birds were sitting on a fence. 5 birds flew away. How many birds are left on the fence?"
  • Place 8 counters on a ten-frame. Physically remove 5 while narrating "take from."
  • Introduce the equation: 8 – 5 = 3. Point to each number: start amount, amount taken, amount left (difference).
  • Ask 2–3 students to retell the story in their own words using the equation.
Ask: "What does the 8 tell us? What does the 5 tell us? What does 3 tell us?"
12–20 min
Guided Practice Game

Game: "Take-From Theater"

Small groups of 3–4 act out teacher-given take-from stories using classroom objects (counters, cubes, or stand-in "characters").

  • Teacher reads a take-from story card aloud (e.g., "9 apples in a basket. 4 are eaten. How many are left?").
  • One student in each group acts it out with counters while the others write the matching equation on a mini whiteboard.
  • On "Show me!" all groups hold up their whiteboard equation and answer.
  • Rotate the "actor" role each round; play 3–4 rounds.
Prompt for struggling groups: "What number did we start with? What happened next?"
20–27 min
Independent/Partner Practice Game

Game: "Roll and Take Away"

Partners play together, giving hands-on repeated practice with self-checking.

  • Each pair gets 10 counters, one die, and a mini whiteboard.
  • Partner A starts with all 10 counters on the ten-frame mat.
  • Partner B rolls the die and removes that many counters, saying "take away ___."
  • Both partners write the equation (10 – roll = left) and say the difference together.
  • Reset to 10 and swap roles; repeat for the full practice time.
Challenge option: start from a partner-chosen number (not always 10) for an extra layer of thinking.
27–30 min
Closing / Quick Check

Exit Ticket: "Show What You Know"

Fast formative check before transitioning.

  • On a whiteboard or slip of paper: "6 pencils in a cup. 2 pencils are taken out. How many are left? Draw it and write the equation."
  • Students hold up their answer for a quick teacher scan.
  • One volunteer shares their equation and explains what each number means.

🎯 Differentiation & Support

Extra Support

  • Keep numbers within 5 during the games
  • Use a physical ten-frame mat for every story
  • Pair with a peer buddy during "Roll and Take Away"

Enrichment

  • Use two dice and subtract the smaller roll from the larger
  • Ask students to write their own take-from story for a partner to solve
  • Introduce missing-number equations (10 – ? = 4)

Math Talk Questions

  • "How do you know some were taken away?"
  • "Could you solve this a different way?"
  • "What would change if we started with a bigger number?"

Assessment Look-Fors

  • Correctly identifies start, change, and result
  • Writes a matching subtraction equation
  • Can explain the meaning of the difference

🧰 Quick Reference: Materials Checklist

  • Counters (10 per student)
  • Ten-frame mats
  • Mini whiteboards + markers
  • 1 die per pair
  • Take-from story cards
  • Exit ticket slips
